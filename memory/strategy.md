# Trading Strategy

**STATUS: ACTIVE**
**Initialized:** 2026-05-20

---

## 1. Thesis

The macro backdrop as of May 2026 favors selective, quality-first stock picking
over broad index exposure:

- **Earnings momentum is strong** — Q1 2026 S&P 500 earnings grew ~15% YoY,
  with 78% of companies beating estimates. Beat-and-raise names offer the
  clearest alpha.
- **Sector rotation is real** — defense (+38% YTD), energy (+21.5% YTD), and
  materials (+17.6% YTD) are crushing the index. Tech is mixed; AI
  infrastructure names still work but broad tech is fading.
- **Rates declining slowly** — Fed funds at 3.5–3.75%; Fed Chair leadership
  transition (Powell term ended May 15) introduces modest policy uncertainty.
  1–2 more rate cuts expected in 2026, which favors quality growth and dividend
  growers.
- **Valuation is elevated** — S&P 500 forward P/E ~20.9x, above the 10-yr
  average of 18.9x. This limits multiple expansion; earnings growth must carry
  returns. Avoids names with stretched valuations and no earnings momentum.
- **Edge vs. SPY**: overweight defense, energy, AI infrastructure, and select
  healthcare. Underweight consumer discretionary, commercial real estate, and
  broad tech ETFs. SPY is near all-time highs (~$738); beating it requires
  deliberate sector bets and tight loss discipline.

## 2. Universe

- US large- and mid-cap stocks listed on NYSE or Nasdaq
- Price ≥ $5 at entry (no penny stocks)
- Liquid: average daily volume ≥ 1M shares or large notional
- No options, no shorting, no margin, no leverage, no crypto
- ADRs allowed if underlying is a quality global business (e.g., TSM)
- Broad ETFs allowed for diversification if sector thesis warrants, but SPY
  itself is excluded (goal is to beat it, not hold it)

---

## 3. Entry Signals

Open a new position only when **at least three** of the following apply:

1. **Earnings momentum**: beat + raise in the most recent earnings report; or
   guidance raised mid-quarter
2. **Sector tailwind**: operating in a sector with clear macro support (defense
   spending, AI capex, energy demand, GLP-1/healthcare)
3. **Reasonable valuation**: PEG ratio < 2.0, or P/E materially below sector
   peers, or forward P/E < 25x for a quality compounder
4. **Technical footing**: stock not in a clear downtrend; ideally near a
   consolidation base or recent breakout
5. **Identifiable catalyst**: new contract/backlog win, FDA approval, guidance
   raise, activist involvement, or restructuring announcement

Write a one-paragraph thesis before every entry. If you can't write it clearly,
don't enter.

---

## 4. Sizing

- **Starter position**: 7–10% of portfolio equity at entry
- **Full conviction**: up to 15% (reserve the 20% cap only for adding to
  existing winners)
- Never open a new position at more than 10% of equity on the same day as
  another new position
- Prefer whole-share quantities so trailing stops are always possible
- Build the portfolio gradually: 3 new positions per week max per the guardrails

---

## 5. Exit Signals

- **Trailing stop triggers** (10% — placed immediately after every fill)
- **Midday −7% rule** (close any position >7% below entry at 12:30 check)
- **Thesis broken**: guidance cut, competitive displacement, sector reversal,
  or the specific catalyst proved wrong
- **Stretched valuation + decelerating growth**: if forward P/E expands to >2x
  sector peers with no acceleration in revenue/earnings growth, trim or exit
- **Concentration risk**: if a winner grows to >18% of portfolio, trim back
  toward 15%

---

## 6. Cash Policy

- **Minimum**: 5% cash at all times (hard guardrail)
- **Target**: 10–15% cash during normal operations (dry powder for
  opportunities and to cushion drawdowns)
- **Raise cash** above 20% if: market makes a sustained break below the 50-day
  moving average, credit spreads widen materially, or 3+ held positions hit
  their stops in the same week
- **Deploy cash** when high-conviction setups appear within the weekly 3-position
  and 25%-daily-deployment guardrails
