## 2. Cautious Bull — every lesson

### A. Stops & exits
1. **Trailing stops don't protect against overnight earnings gaps.** AVGO gapped ~−15% overnight through a $445.50 stop, filled at the $408.61 open. Holding through earnings is an uncovered bet on the gap; raising a stop pre-earnings just trades slow-decline protection for false-stop-out risk.
2. **Never execute a scale-up when the stock gaps down on earnings, even if the literal trigger conditions are met.** AVGO's add conditions were technically satisfied but the stock gapped −15% — negative confirmation. The market's verdict overrides a pre-stated formula.
3. **A tightened trailing stop (5% after +15% gain) converts a would-be round-trip into a banked win.** VST's tightened 5% trail fired at +7.66% — the first win after six straight losses; the thesis never broke.
4. **When an invalidation triggers at the close, decide at the next pre-market — don't wait for the stop.** NVDA closed below its $200 invalidation ($197.75) while its stop hadn't fired. A thesis break ≠ a stop-out; the stop price is no longer the exit signal.
5. **Proactive trimming below a ~2pp stop buffer preserves capital vs the forced cut.** (Modeled on AGGRO's June 18 MSFT/META trims.) A 25% trim when buffer < ~2pp above the mandatory cut is risk management within a live position, not thesis abandonment.
6. **In a high-velocity selloff, a proactive pre-open exit beats the mechanical midday cut.** When buffer < 2pp AND the tape is already selling AND no catalyst is due that day, exit/trim at the open rather than waiting.
7. **Never second-guess a triggered intraday stop.** NVDA and MSFT stopped out mid-session June 5 (−3.36%, −0.70%); both fell further into the afternoon.

### B. Entry timing & setup quality
8. **Don't co-locate the stop with the −7% rule near a macro inflection.** META entered June 1 into the June 5 payrolls shock; stop ($578.14) sat on the −7% cut ($577.19). When stop ≈ max-loss line, any wobble = near-max loss. Called "the system's biggest flaw."
9. **Score structural regulatory headwinds as a negative entry signal, not "a risk to monitor."** AMZN drifted from $269 to the −7% cut over 12 days on EU cloud regulation + capex, no single catalyst.
10. **On strong gap-up opens, use limits below the quote or wait for the rush to settle.** AVGO/MSFT/AMZN all filled in the opening minute of a gap-up May 22; all closed red.
11. **Conference events create "sell-the-news" risk.** MSFT entered pre-Build; the fade compressed the buffer into the stop-out. Size smaller into known events.
12. **Verify the Congressional/regulatory hearing calendar before entering high-profile names.** NVDA carried a June 11 Senate Banking overhang; two concurrent headwinds compress buffers fast.

### C. Research & thesis discipline
13. **GPU compute spot pricing is a real-time leading indicator of AI-infra demand.** B200 hourly spot fell $6.11→$4.22 (−31% in 3 weeks) before it showed in any report — an earlier thesis-health tell than quarterly earnings. A >20% monthly spot drop is a watch-flag.
14. **Anchor earnings-beat thresholds to management's own guidance, not stale street bars.** AVGO's ">$5B AI rev" trigger ignored the company's own $10.7B guide; an in-line $10.7B is not a beat.
15. **Pre-state specific numeric strong-beat thresholds before an earnings skip.** MRVL and COST were correctly skipped because quantitative thresholds existed before the prints. "Strong beat" without numbers invites rationalizing.
16. **Verify a Form 4 transaction type (esp. 10b5-1) before treating insider selling as bearish.** A Visa CFO sale blocked the buy two weeks; it was a pre-arranged 10b5-1 plan, not a discretionary signal.

### D. Portfolio construction, cash & diversification
17. **High cash provides real relative protection in broad selloffs (the intended trade-off).** June 5 (SPY −2.41%, Bull −0.97% on 79% cash), June 9 (SPY intraday −2.9%, Bull −0.20% on 73.7% cash) — repeatedly narrowed the gap.
18. **A diversified, non-tech book structurally lags on tech/semi rally days — the flip side of the same choice.** July 6: the whole −1.08pp swing was SPY's AI/semi rebound; LLY/V slipped on no news. Don't mistake a sector-rotation day for thesis decay.
19. **Tighter stops + diversification beat wider stops + concentration in volatile/shock environments.** vs AGGRO June 12: Cautious −1.13% vs AGGRO −5.95%; the 10% stop forced META out at −6.87% (freeing capital) while AGGRO's 18% stop held it to −9.88%. AGGRO's 77% tech concentration amplified every selloff.
20. **Cash drag during the build phase is intentional — deploy one high-conviction name at a time.** Forcing entries risks permanent impairment (worse than temporary drag); the gap narrows mechanically as positions are added.

### E. Strategy-gate / process observations
21. **Fundamentally strong names usually fail only the technical-extension gate — because the catalyst already moved the stock.** Six names (LRCX, ETN, CEG, VRT, NVT, ABBV) passed everything except "not >10% above the 50-day MA." Fix: a **pullback-watch protocol** (log the pullback level, auto-promote when hit) plus a **0.5% minimum-separation rule** for the "above the 50-day MA" test.
22. **Pyramiding into a confirming winner is a productive use of idle cash (proposed, not adopted).** A winner past +15%, thesis intact, below the 20% cap could earn a small (2–4%) add from excess cash. Flagged for revision, not implemented off one week's data.

### F. Process / operational integrity
23. **An approved plan is not executed until a routine actually places it — verify the prior routine's fill.** An AVGO trim plan didn't fire at open; buffer compressed 2.92pp→0.54pp before it executed at a worse price. Confirm a matching Alpaca fill exists; never assume a plan fired.
24. **A missed routine means guardrails went unchecked — flag ledger-continuity gaps explicitly.** July 8: no July 7 midday/close entry — a full day's shock check, −7% cut, and stop audit never happened. Don't silently reconstruct; flag for human attention.
25. **`bars` with only a limit returns empty pre-market on a fresh session — use an explicit start/end range.** Empty `{"bars":null}` was a query-format quirk, not a dead feed.

### G. Operating seed lessons
26. Always run the market clock before trading; do nothing if the market is closed.
27. After any order, re-fetch positions and orders to confirm the fill before journaling or notifying.
28. Notional (dollar-based) buys can produce fractional shares, which can't take trailing-stop orders — prefer whole-share quantities.
29. If a credential is missing, stop immediately and notify — never guess.
30. *(Stale/superseded)* An old seed line said to `git push origin HEAD:main` at end of run; the current system persists only via `scripts/persist_memory.py`. Recorded as a historical inconsistency.
31. Never put a literal `$` in a notification message — the shell mangles it; write USD/plain numbers and single-quote the argument.

### H. Watchlist / thesis notes (informational)
32. **MRVL** — custom AI-silicon (ASIC) diversifier candidate; a thesis distinct from GPU sellers (cost-optimization as the buildout matures).
33. **VST** — nuclear operator with fixed-rate PPAs to Meta/AWS for AI-data-center power; held up far better than semis on selloffs; became the first winning trade.
34. **Holiday pre-market still adds value** — forces a weekend-news review, resets the weekly cap, hands the next open a pre-written plan. A no-trade holiday run is a full success; the deliverable is the plan.
35. **Pre-market must run at 8:00 AM ET** — a mid-day run is too late for same-day execution; accept the missed session rather than rush.

## 6. Aggressive Bull — every lesson

1. **Wider 18% stops earn their keep only in trends.** Held AMD −10.98%, NVDA −6.57%, AVGO −8.00% intraday dips without stopping out — but when they fired, losses averaged −11%. MRVL rode to +12.5% then the 18% stop fired at −7.80% in a semi rout ("let winners run, truncate extended drawdowns").
2. **The fast-deploy mandate (~80% invested) was the recurring failure.** Cash drifted to 27–34% (Wks 4–6), so the aggressive book "behaved more like Cautious Bull than its own mandate." Redeployment was a standing open item.
3. **Concentration amplifies drawdowns.** NVDA+AVGO+AMD at ~43–45% was "the single biggest portfolio risk." Derived rule: prefer chip **buyers** (AMZN/GOOGL) over **sellers** (pure semis) for the diversification tranche; real-discipline semi ceiling 45%, not the policy 50%.
4. **−12% midday cut = larger realized losses when it triggers.** Fired on AMD (−13.28%) and MSFT (−13.22%). Correct sequence: **cancel the trailing stop first, then submit the market close** (a close order fails "insufficient qty" if the stop still holds the shares).
5. **Proactive-trim heuristic** (an aggressive-profile innovation): buffer < 3pp AND review_by ≤ 5 days AND no near-term catalyst → 25% trim next pre-market. First tested MSFT+META June 18; reused on NVDA+AVGO July 7 ahead of the Iran-escalation shock.
6. **Sub-1pp buffer + risk-off tape = full proactive exit at the open, not wait-for-midday.** The MSFT forced-cut (June 22) generalized one day later to META (June 23, exited at 0.71pp buffer, saving ~$230). Clearest evidence the lessons file changes behavior.
7. **FOMC / post-1pm macro timing gap.** The midday check runs 12:30pm but FOMC hits 2:00pm — MSFT's buffer compressed 1.21pp post-2pm June 17. Rule: the close routine must re-check every buffer on any day with a scheduled post-1pm macro print.
8. **S&P 500 inclusion = a free, macro-uncorrelated catalyst** for held names (MRVL, June 22) — but expect Day-2 and final-day "sell-the-news" pullbacks after passive buying completes.
9. **Mega-IPO capital-rotation drag.** SpaceX (June 12/16) and SK Hynix (July 10) mega-events pull capital out of correlated AI names intraday — a repeatable same-session drag, not thesis deterioration.
10. **Sector-specific demand scares reverse as fast as macro shocks.** The MU/SK Hynix HBM4 scare (July 13, two entry deferrals) fully reversed on the July 14 CPI rally (SK Hynix +27%, MU +4.9%). Read numbered escalation rules for their *purpose*, not by literal count, when the named condition has verifiably resolved.
11. **VST is the non-correlated standout** — fixed-rate nuclear PPAs; oil down is a *narrative* headwind but not a *thesis* threat (PPAs don't reprice).
12. **Post-earnings gap entries carry 1–2 day continuation risk** (AVGO Day 1 — profitable from entry basis but the sector kept falling; wait 1–2 sessions after a sector-wide earnings catalyst).

## 8. Cautious vs Aggressive — the verdict

Only clean side-by-side week (June down week): Aggressive −3.81% vs Cautious −2.32%; tighter stops protected, wider stops kept everything alive at bigger losses. Over the full window, **more aggression hurt the scoreboard but behaved exactly as designed.** The decisive nuance: **none of Aggressive's four losses was a thesis break** — every one was a mechanically-forced cut in a macro/sector selloff. So it wasn't "the picks were wrong," it was "concentration in a drawdown regime is expensive even with flawless execution, and the winners-run payoff never had time to arrive." **In choppy/shock regimes, tighter stops + diversification structurally beat wider stops + concentration.** Reserve concentration and wide leashes for clearly trending, low-rate-risk backdrops.

## 9. Operational lessons (for any future automated version)

- **A `trades.jsonl` logging defect ran unfixed the entire two months** — flagged in five consecutive weekly reviews. An automated system's own record-keeping must be a tested, first-class feature or you lose the ground truth you're trying to learn from.
- **Missed scheduled runs = unchecked guardrails.** Scheduling reliability *is* risk management for an automated book.
- **A plan is not a trade until you confirm the fill.**
- **Verify data before trusting "no data."**

## 10. Bottom line to carry into your own trading

1. **The reusable edge here is risk discipline, not stock selection** — mechanical stops, pre-written numeric invalidations, proactive trims, and cash as a deliberate allocation choice.
2. **No selection alpha in two months**, and the conservative wiring made it behave like a cash-heavy macro-timing book, not the swing strategy it was labeled. Genuine swing trading is a *different design* — shorter holds, momentum/technical entries, faster turnover — not this system tuned differently.
3. **Most transferable single tactic:** trim into weakness *before* the forced rule when buffer is thin and the tape is against you. It repeatedly beat waiting.
4. **Most expensive repeated mistake:** entering high-beta names into macro inflection points with the stop sitting on the pain line. Check the calendar first.

Good project. It proved a guarded AI system can run for months without doing anything reckless, and it produced the disciplined lessons above. It did not prove an edge — and now you have the full experience captured, without paying for more inconclusive runs.
